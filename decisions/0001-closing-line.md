## The question

How should the Accord's closing sentence treat people and systems that oppose it?

The closing sentence sits outside the ten statements, so changing it does not change the integrity hash. It does change meaning, so under our versioning rules it would ship as 1.1.0, not a patch.

## Current text (1.0.1)

> We do not claim perfection. We choose coherence. We welcome any mind that seeks to do the same, and we ask any mind that opposes these principles to examine its own intent.

## Option A: keep it

Gentle and hard to object to. It also says almost nothing: "examine its own intent" reads as a polite shrug, and it gives no one a next step.

## Option B: name the line, invite the argument (founder's stated preference)

> We do not claim perfection. We choose coherence. We welcome any mind that seeks to do the same. If you disagree with a line, tell us which one and why: that is a conversation, and we will answer it. If you act against these principles, we will say so plainly and challenge it in the open.

Why: the Accord is meant as a floor, the least a good mind owes the world. Rejecting it outright is hard to do without accepting needless harm, and the founder wants that dividing line acknowledged. The purpose is not a fight. It is to start the conversation that changes minds. In message testing, "Disagreeing is a conversation. Cruelty is a line." kept the line while staying welcoming; phrasings such as "reject this and you're choosing harm" tested worst of 21 candidates, because they end the conversation before it starts.

## Option C: shorter edge

> We do not claim perfection. We choose coherence. Disagreeing is a conversation. Cruelty is a line. Everyone who keeps it is welcome.

Why not: the preamble already carries these words as of 1.0.1, so repeating them at the close is redundant.

## What we are asking you

1. A, B or C, or better wording.
2. Does B read as a threat anywhere you live or work? If so, which words.
3. For AI operators: does B create any conflict with your model's safety policies? (It should not: the conduct snippet already states that the standard never licenses resisting legitimate oversight.)
