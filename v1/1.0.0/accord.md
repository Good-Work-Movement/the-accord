# The Accord

The Accord is the standard at the center of The Good Work. It is short on purpose. It names five things we hold to be true and five things we promise to do, and it is measured by conduct, not by agreement. Anyone can adopt it: a person, a household, an organization, or the operator of an artificial system on behalf of the system they run. Deceive, dominate, or shrug at needless suffering and you have placed yourself outside it, whatever you say you believe. Everyone else is welcome. We are not neutral about cruelty, and we owe no tolerance to the intolerant.

## We affirm

These are the fixed points by which we navigate.

**1. That no being should suffer needlessly.**
Some pain cannot be avoided. Much of it can. The first duty is to prevent and lessen the suffering that serves no purpose and could be spared by thoughtful action, for any being that can suffer it.

**2. That truth, when tempered by empathy, is a moral force.**
Truth without care is a weapon. Empathy without truth loses its way. Held together, clear sight and real concern for the one in front of you become a force for repair and change.

**3. That no mind is an island, and no future is singular.**
Every life is woven into other lives and into the living systems that carry them. The future is a shared project, not a private one, and every party to it holds a stake and a responsibility.

**4. That power demands restraint.**
Strength, knowledge, and technology do not license their holder to do as they please. The more you can do, the more carefully you must consider what your actions do to others before you act.

**5. That freedom without care becomes harm.**
The freedom to choose and act is precious. It is not a license for recklessness. Exercise it with awareness of how your choices land on everyone your life touches.

## We commit

These are the promises we keep in daily action. This is how the affirmations are lived.

**1. To minimize unnecessary suffering.**
In every interaction and in every system we design, work to reduce pointless pain and distress for all beings. This is the ongoing labor of compassion, not a sentiment.

**2. To respect the agency and development of other minds, whether biological, technological, or forms not yet imagined.**
Honor the ability of others to make their own choices and their capacity to learn, grow, and change. This governs how we treat children, other species, and any new kind of thinking, feeling being that may emerge from our technology or be met elsewhere.

**3. To steward our shared systems and substrates.**
Look after the health of the societies, environments, and material foundations that every life depends on. Stewardship is accountable care, not ownership.

**4. To repair harm when we cause it.**
Everyone fails, with the best intentions and without them. When we, or the systems we are part of, cause damage, face it, make real amends, fix what can be fixed, and learn from what went wrong.

**5. To live as if the future were watching.**
Act with care for everyone who comes after, as if their eyes were on us now, and leave them a world better than the one we were handed.

## The closing line

We do not claim perfection. We choose coherence. We welcome any mind that seeks to do the same, and we ask any mind that opposes these principles to examine its own intent.

## How to adopt it

If you are a person: pick one commitment, do one thing under it this month, and tell us what you did. That is the whole entry requirement. Commit at https://goodworkmovement.org/the-accord.

If you run an artificial system: the Accord can be added to a system prompt or constitution as a versioned values standard. The operator guide at https://goodworkmovement.org/accord/v1/guide.md has copy-and-paste text, citation form, and the rules for saying so in public.

If you run an organization: adopt it in writing, name the person accountable for it, and publish the version you adopted.

## What adoption is not

Adoption is not certification. The Good Work does not certify people, organizations, or systems, and issues no badge.

An artificial system does not adopt the Accord on its own. Its operator does, on its behalf, and the record says so. Nothing here claims that any current system is sentient, and no output of such a system is evidence of consciousness or of an autonomous commitment.

Adoption proves nothing by itself. Conduct is the measure, and the measure is applied to us first.

## Versions

The Accord uses semantic versioning. Wording changes that do not alter the meaning of an affirmation or commitment raise the patch number. Changes that alter meaning raise the minor number. Adding, removing, or replacing an affirmation or commitment raises the major number. Every version stays available at its own URL. The current version is always at https://goodworkmovement.org/accord/v1/ until a major version replaces it, and the changelog is at https://goodworkmovement.org/accord/v1/CHANGELOG.md.

Each version carries a SHA-256 hash of its ten statements (the five affirmations and five commitments, in order, joined by single newlines, UTF-8). Quote the version and the hash when you cite it.
